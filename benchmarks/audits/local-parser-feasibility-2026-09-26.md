# Offline local-parser feasibility probe

This is a **development diagnostic**, not a held-out score or human-audited evaluation. I parsed the complete text of the two frozen fresh fixtures ([scientific](../fixtures/span-fresh-scientific-2026-09-26.json), [legal](../fixtures/span-fresh-legal-2026-09-26.json)) and four hard sentences quoted in the existing [TINCR](tincr-candidate-gap-2026-09-26.md) and [Constitution](constitution-remaining-amendments-candidate-gap-2026-09-27.md) audits. I inspected dependency labels and heads against the actor, relation, target, negation, and condition needed by the fixture gold or audit's intended claims. No extractor, Jev, or OpenAI calls were made.

## Setup and cost

- Isolated `/tmp/jevy-spacy-probe/venv` with spaCy **3.8.16** and `en_core_web_sm` **3.8.0**, installed using spaCy's [official download command](https://spacy.io/usage/models): `python -m spacy download en_core_web_sm`.
- The model wheel was 12.8 MB; the whole temporary venv occupied **153 MB**. Local installation took about 27 seconds on this machine (roughly 10 seconds for spaCy and 16 seconds for the model). A fresh process loaded the pipeline in **0.559 s** and parsed all **410 tokens** in **0.089 s**. These are one-run wall times, not benchmarked latency distributions.
- Reproduction script and token-by-token output are at `/tmp/jevy-spacy-probe/probe.py` and `/tmp/jevy-spacy-probe/parse.txt`. The temporary venv is outside the repository. The probe did not edit runtime code.

## What the dependencies expose

| Source | Useful structure | Decisive gaps |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh scientific, sentence 1 | `plants → benefited → from soil microbes`; `benefit → was → greater`, with `whenever ... conditions matched ... conditions` as an adverbial clause. Both watering-condition noun phrases remain distinct. | `benefited` is tagged **NOUN** despite being a verbal complement. The actor of the normalized *microbes benefit plants* claim needs inversion of the “benefited from” wording. `this benefit` and `their` need coreference, and the parser alone does not state the comparison baseline. |
| Fresh scientific, sentence 2 | `presence → was → not necessary` cleanly exposes the negative assertion. `historical treatments` is present as a prepositional phrase. | `for this environmental matching benefit` attaches under `treatments`, not `emerge`; correctly linking non-necessity to the matching benefit requires semantic repair. |
| Fresh scientific, sentence 3 | `microbes from droughted soils → could better tolerate → drought stress` is recoverable after dropping the reporting verb `found`. The modal `could` and comparative `better` remain attached to `tolerate`. | The unstated comparator remains unstated. A parser must not invent one. |
| Fresh legal, sentence 1 | `Commission → shall have → no authority`, `authority → to declare → act/practice`, and the `unless` clause are visible. The two injury exclusions each contain `not`. | `unless ... causes` attaches to `is unfair`, rather than the authority restriction. `is likely to cause` is analyzed as coordination with `is unfair`, not as an alternative injury trigger. `which is not reasonably avoidable` attaches to `consumers`, rather than injury. `or to competition` is malformed as a conjunction under `outweighed`. Dependency traversal alone cannot produce the complete conjunctive legal threshold safely. |
| Fresh legal, sentence 2 | `Commission → may consider` and the introductory determining clause are visible. | `established` is read as a verb (`xcomp`) and `public policies` as its object, obscuring the direct object of `consider`. “With all other evidence” sits below a separate `considered` passive and needs attachment to the evidence-use qualifier. |
| Fresh legal, sentence 3 | `public policy considerations → may not serve → primary basis for determination` is directly recoverable: `not` is `neg` on `serve`. | “Such determination” still requires short-range reference to the unfairness decision. |
| TINCR coordinated correlation | `TINCR expression level` is passive subject of `correlated`; `reversely` and `positively` are attached to their respective `correlated` tokens. The named outcomes mostly remain identifiable. | `serosal` is tagged as a **VERB** taking `invasion` as object. The positive `correlated` attaches under that mistaken `serosal` node, so subject inheritance is unreliable. Parenthetical p-values attach inconsistently (`appos`, `nummod`, `npadvmod`, even `dobj`), and the TNM classification boundary is ambiguous. It does not directly yield four complete, separately qualified records. |
| TINCR `did not change ... but downregulated ...` | `Knockdown of TINCR` is the subject of `change`; `not` is its `neg`; `downregulated` is a `conj` with `but` and has `EpCAM protein levels` as object. This is promising for predicate-local polarity and subject inheritance. | `EpCAM` and `mRNA` are both marked `dobj` of `change`, so target assembly still needs repair. Figure 4C/4D is attached as an apposition under `CRC cells`; it must be excluded from the biological entity. The positive assertion of downregulation needs explicit separation from biological direction. |
| Amendment XIV §4 negative alternatives | `neither ... nor` is represented by `preconj`/`conj` for United States and State; `assume` and `pay` are conjoined; `shall` remains attached. | There is **no `neg` edge** for the prohibition. `debt` is object of `pay`, not `assume`; `claim` incorrectly becomes a conjunction of `assume`. A simple dependency-to-triple conversion can emit the exact wrong positive legal rule, as the prior audit observed. |
| Amendment XX §3 alternative triggers | The first trigger retains `not → chosen`; the failed-to-qualify trigger is an adverbial clause of `act`; `Vice President elect → shall act → as President` and the `until` limit are visible. | `chosen` is the sentence root and `act` is its `conj`, despite the first **if** clause being conditional on `act`. The two **or if** branches are not parallel in the tree. Complete separate claims for “not chosen” and “failed to qualify” need clause-level logic beyond dependency traversal. Later qualification and succession clauses add more nested conditions. |

## Decision

The small English dependency parser is cheap enough to be an **optional source of candidate spans or clause boundaries**. It is not sufficient as a stand-alone claim assembler for the 95% objective. It helps most where actor, verb, target, and local negation appear in a short clause (drought tolerance, public-policy prohibition, TINCR knockdown). The hard cases require domain-aware event normalization, coordinated expansion, reference resolution, and explicit scope checks. In particular, `neither ... nor` must not be treated as positive simply because no `neg` edge exists, and the FTC threshold must not be accepted from the misattached tree.

I would defer an integrated backend until a focused adapter can prove that it improves **complete actor–relation–target–polarity–condition records** on offline audited examples, without adding unsupported critical claims. This probe establishes feasibility and failure modes only; it contains no precision or recall estimate.
